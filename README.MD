Study title:
Host-response phenotypes and piperacillin pharmacokinetics in critically ill adults with respiratory infection: a prospective population pharmacokinetic study

Short title:
TDM-TIME

Registration:
https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05971979

Study objective:
The aim of this prospective observational population pharmacokinetic study of critically ill patients with lower respiratory infection was to develop an exploratory pipeline to assess the impact of established and newly characterised immune phenotypes and corresponding clinical variables on describing pharmacokinetic variability and therapeutic target attainment of piperacillin.

Abstract:
Sepsis is characterised by marked biological and physiological heterogeneity that may contribute to variability in antimicrobial pharmacokinetics and target attainment in critically ill patients. We conducted a prospective observational population pharmacokinetic study to determine whether molecular immune phenotypes explain variability in piperacillin disposition in adults admitted to intensive care with acute lower respiratory infection. Participants receiving intravenous piperacillin/tazobactam underwent serial serum drug concentration measurement alongside whole-blood RNA sequencing and multiplex cytokine profiling. Immune phenotypes were defined using established Sepsis Response Signature and hyper-/hypo-inflammatory classifiers and unsupervised multi-omic factor analysis, targeting unexplained pharmacokinetic variability. Nonlinear mixed-effects modelling with stepwise covariate selection was used to identify determinants of pharmacokinetic variability. Of 32 participants enrolled, 30 were evaluable and 24 contributed 94 samples to pharmacokinetic analysis. A two-compartment model best described piperacillin disposition. Observed exposure was highly variable: 42% (10/24) had trough concentrations below 16 mg/L and 8% (2/24) exceeded 160 mg/L. The final model included eGFR, C-reactive protein (CRP), and transcriptomic Factor 4 as covariates on clearance; higher eGFR increased clearance, whereas higher CRP and Factor 4 reduced clearance. Established SRS and hyper-/hypo-inflammatory phenotypes did not improve the model. At an MIC of 16 mg/L with intermittent dosing, probability of target attainment (PTA) for 100%fT>4×MIC varied markedly across covariate-stratified scenarios, ranging from 0% to 94%; PTA was 4% versus 42% for high versus low eGFR, 94% versus 2% for high versus low Factor 4, and 53% versus 0% for high versus low CRP. These findings demonstrate that renal function, systemic inflammation, and a transcriptomic host-response phenotype influence piperacillin clearance variability. Targeting unexplained pharmacokinetic variability with dimension-reduced molecular phenotypes may provide a framework for biologically informed precision beta-lactam dosing.
